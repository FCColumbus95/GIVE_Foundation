## Federal disclosures versus recommended website practices
Federal tax law generally requires public inspection/copy access to specified records; it does not impose one universal website footer or require every charity to post every record online. A transparency page is a practical way to provide access but must have real documents or a functioning request process behind it. Handle requests within applicable rules and deadlines; generally in-person copies at request and written copies within 30 days, subject to applicable exceptions.

For each single gift of $250 or more, the donor needs a contemporaneous written acknowledgment to claim a deduction. Build acknowledgments for all gifts as an operating practice. Include receiving organization name, cash amount or description (not charity-assigned value) of noncash property, and whether goods/services were provided; if so, describe and give a good-faith value estimate, unless an applicable exception applies. A generic website notice is not a gift acknowledgment.

For a payment exceeding $75 that is partly a gift and partly for goods/services, the charity generally must give a written quid pro quo disclosure explaining that only the excess over benefits is deductible and providing a good-faith benefit estimate, subject to exceptions. The $75 threshold is the total payment, not the deductible portion.

Public charity donor identities are generally protected from annual-return disclosure; private foundation rules differ. Do not publish unredacted donor information without applying the correct classification and disclosure rules. Do not remove information that the law requires to be public.

An EIN and 501(c)(3) statement are useful for transparency once verified; an EIN alone does not establish exemption or deductibility. Do not use “application pending” unless an application actually has been filed and that description is accurate.

Privacy, donor rights, accessibility, refund, and governance pages are not all universal federal 501(c)(3) website mandates. Their legal requirements depend on operations, jurisdictions, and data practices. WCAG 2.2 AA is a useful accessibility target, not a certification established by adding an accessibility statement. A cookie banner should reflect actual technology and applicable law, not be added as decoration.

## Receipt templates — fill only with verified facts
Pure cash gift:
“[Verified legal name] acknowledges your contribution of $[amount] received on [date]. No goods or services were provided in exchange for this contribution.”
Add EIN, donor details, and transaction reference after verification. Use the no-benefits sentence only when true.

Payment with benefits:
“Your payment was $[total]. Goods or services provided had an estimated fair market value of $[value]: [description]. The amount deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over that value, subject to applicable law.”
Do not promise the donor a particular tax result.

Noncash gift:
“Acknowledgment of property received on [date]: [description]. [Accurate goods/services statement].”
Do not assign the donor’s claimed value.

## Governance and international operations
Confirm articles/bylaws, charitable purposes and dissolution clauses, conflict-of-interest procedures, compensation and related-party approvals, financial controls, document retention, whistleblower process, and accurate reporting. Several are recommended governance practices rather than universal standalone website mandates. Do not publish draft policies as already adopted.

Review the separately governed enterprise for related-party transactions, private benefit/inurement, unrelated business income, and correct accounting. A 501(c)(3) organization must not participate or intervene in campaigns for or against political candidates; lobbying restrictions depend on applicable rules.

For Jamaica/Africa work, distinguish the U.S. legal recipient from foreign branches, grantees, and businesses. Verify local registrations, agreements, charitable use, oversight and reporting. A U.S. organization supporting foreign work must retain appropriate control over donated funds; earmarked gifts to foreign entities generally do not receive the same U.S. deduction treatment. Private foundations may need equivalency determination or expenditure responsibility. Do not characterize charitable gifts as business investment returns.

Before youth program registration or publication of identifiable child images, implement safeguarding, parent/guardian consent, appropriate data minimization, and separate participation terms. Do not assume COPPA applies automatically to every nonprofit; assess the actual entity and activities.

Confirm applicable annual IRS return/notice and state filing calendars. Missing required IRS annual filings for three consecutive years generally causes automatic revocation. Website edits do not cure missed filings or registrations.
